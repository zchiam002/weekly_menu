# Taiwanese Beef Noodle Soup 红烧牛肉面

> Beef shank braised with doubanjiang, tomato paste and rice wine, simmered with tomato, apple and a five-spice bag, then strained to a clear, rich broth and ladled over wheat noodles with bok choy.

**Servings:** 6.5 (source recipe serves 4 — quantities below are pre-scaled ×1.625)

## Ingredients

### Blanching & broth base

- 1.3–1.5kg beef shank, cut into large cubes
- 8 slices ginger
- ~3.25L water (the strained blanching liquid becomes the broth base)

### Aromatics & sauce

- 3–4 tbsp cooking oil
- 1½ white onions, sliced
- 1½ bulbs garlic, cloves separated and lightly crushed
- 6–7 shallots
- 3–4 slices ginger
- 5 stalks scallions / spring onions
- 3¼ tbsp soy bean paste (doubanjiang)
- 3¼ tbsp tomato paste
- ~160ml (⅔ cup) rice wine (michiu or Shaoxing)
- 5 tbsp light soy sauce
- 8 small pieces rock sugar

### Broth additions

- 1½–2 tomatoes, quartered
- 1 apple, quartered

### Spice bag

- ~1½ tsp Sichuan peppercorns
- ~1½ tsp fennel seeds
- 1½–2 cinnamon sticks
- 8 cloves
- 1½–2 star anise

### To serve

- 650–800g white wheat noodles, cooked / blanched
- 6–7 heads bok choy, blanched
- 1½–2 tbsp sesame oil, for the bowls
- Ground pepper, to taste
- Chopped scallions and fresh cilantro, to garnish

## Method

1. **Blanch the beef:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add the 8 slices of ginger and the cubed beef shank and boil until the impurities and scum float to the surface. Skim off all the scum, then lift out the beef and set aside. Strain the blanching water through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean pot or jug — this is the broth base.
2. **Sauté aromatics:** Heat the cooking oil in a large heavy-bottomed Dutch oven or deep soup pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion, crushed garlic, shallots, ginger slices and scallion stalks and stir-fry a few minutes until fragrant and softened.
3. **Build the sauce:** Add the soy bean paste and tomato paste and mix until fully incorporated and aromatic.
4. **Deglaze and coat:** Pour in the rice wine and light soy sauce and add the rock sugar. Stir continuously until the sugar begins to dissolve. Return the blanched beef to the pot and keep stir-frying until every piece is evenly coated in the sauce.
5. **Simmer:** Pour in the reserved strained broth until the beef is fully submerged. Add the quartered tomatoes and apple. Tie the Sichuan peppercorns, fennel seeds, cinnamon, cloves and star anise into a spice pouch and submerge it in the broth. Cover, reduce to low and simmer gently for 1.5 hours, until the beef is tender.
6. **Strain:** Remove and discard the spice bag. Scoop out the tender beef and set aside. Pour the remaining soup through a fine-mesh strainer into a second pot to remove the aromatics, onion and fruit, leaving a clear, rich broth.
7. **Assemble:** In each serving bowl put a drizzle of sesame oil (~1 tsp) and a dash of pepper. Add the cooked noodles, then arrange the beef shank and blanched bok choy on top. Pour the piping-hot broth over, garnish with chopped scallions and cilantro, and serve hot.
