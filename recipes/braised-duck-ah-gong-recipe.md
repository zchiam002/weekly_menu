# Braised Duck (Ah Gong's Recipe) 卤鸭

> ✅ **FULLY CONFIRMED as of 20 Sep 2026** — every ingredient and the complete method below come
> directly from his own WhatsApp messages across three exchanges. Started as a copy of
> `recipes/braised-duck-teochew.md`; **that base has been almost entirely replaced**, not just
> adjusted — his recipe uses a single named soy sauce, a caramelized-sugar technique, no aromatic
> paste or garlic/old ginger at all, and the duck goes in belly-up rather than breast-down.

**Servings:** not specified by him — likely similar to the Teochew base's 2kg duck / serves ~8,
but unconfirmed.

## Ingredients

### Duck & rub

- 1 whole duck, about 2kg *(size not specified by him — confirm)*
  - ⚠️ **HEAD AND WEBBED FEET MUST BE LEFT ON** (user, 20 Sep) — the duck is braised and served
    whole in this style. **Tell the stall explicitly when buying**, as many vendors remove both
    by default. Also check the pot is big enough to take the duck with the head still attached.
- Salt, for rubbing over the duck *(no quantity given — "rub it on, massage it in")*

### Braising base

- **大華 (Tai Hua) premium soy sauce — 100cc.** ⚠️ This is a SINGLE named soy sauce, not the
  Teochew base's separate dark soy (1 cup) + light soy (½ cup). 大華 appears to be a specific
  brand/product — worth confirming exactly which one so it can be sourced correctly
- **Dark soy sauce, extra — for coating the cleaned duck** (user, 20 Sep). Rubbed over the duck
  after cleaning to build colour, before it goes into the pot. **No measured quantity — just
  enough to coat it, by eye.** This is on top of the 100cc, which is fully spoken for at the
  caramelizing step, so buy with margin to cover both uses.
- **Regular sugar (普通糖) — 1 heaped tablespoon.** NOT rock sugar — this replaces the earlier
  ~3 tbsp rock sugar guess entirely. Caramelized as its own step before the soy sauce goes in
  (see Method)

### Whole spices

- Cinnamon — 4–5 sticks
- Star anise — **7–8 pieces** *(corrected — the Teochew base and this file's earlier draft both
  had just 2; his number is much higher)*
- Sichuan peppercorns — ½ tsp *(the user separately recalled "green Sichuan is good" while
  watching him cook — not explicitly confirmed as green in his text, but not contradicted either)*
- **NOT toasted — they go in raw, straight into the pot** (user, 20 Sep). This matches his own
  text exactly: "香料直接放在鍋裡" ("the spices go directly into the pot"). An earlier draft of
  this file had a toasting step; it has been removed.
- **Placement confirmed: they go into the pot at the same time as the duck** (Method step 3), per
  his direct text — not earlier, alongside the soy sauce.

### Aromatics

- **Blue ginger (galangal) — 1 piece, about half a fist in size.** His own description —
  supersedes the earlier 70g / 6–8cm estimate entirely. **Stuffed WHOLE into the duck's cavity**,
  not pounded into a paste
- ✅ **CONFIRMED: no old ginger, no garlic.** His own words: "沒有老姜和蒜頭" — "no old ginger and
  no garlic." Fully resolved, removed from this recipe for good.
- ✅ **CONFIRMED: no spring onion.** His own words: "鴨肚只放藍羌" — "the duck's belly only has
  blue ginger in it." The cavity stuffing is galangal alone.

### Water

- A big bowl of boiling water, added right after the soy sauce (into the caramel mixture)
- More water added to the pot once the duck is in, until the level reaches **halfway up the
  duck's body** — some of this water is also poured directly into the duck's cavity at the same
  time

### Offal — all three confirmed

- **Gizzard** — goes in **after 30 minutes** of cooking, i.e. at the very first flip (Method
  step 5). His own words: "鴨胗可以在30分鐘後放"
- **Liver** — cooks for only **10 minutes**, so it goes in roughly 10 minutes before the duck is
  done (around the ~63-minute mark of the ~70–75 minute total — doesn't land exactly on a
  described flip point, so use the 10-minutes-before-done rule rather than a specific flip
  number). His own words: "鴨肝只要煮十分鐘。煮完放在鍋裡。" — once cooked, it's left sitting in
  the pot (not removed) until serving
- **Heart — CONFIRMED as part of the recipe** (his cleaning instructions for it imply it's used).
  **Timing: goes in with the gizzard at the 30-minute mark.** ⚠️ **This is provisional — my
  recommendation, not his instruction.** Reasoning: heart and gizzard are both muscle and behave
  the same way in a braise, so ~40 minutes leaves it tender. Unlike liver it has no narrow
  window — it won't go grainy from extra time; the failure mode is toughness from too little.
  Still worth confirming with him.

**Cleaning, per his own instructions** ("鴨胗內部的黃色的那層皮一定要去掉。肝和胗都用鹽搓幾回。用水
洗乾淨。心可以擠出裡面的血就不用切開來。"):
- **Gizzard:** the yellow inner lining must definitely be removed.
- **Liver and gizzard, both:** rub with salt several times, then rinse clean with water.
- **Heart:** squeeze the blood out from inside — no need to cut it open.

## Method

**Total braise time: ~70–75 minutes** — his own words are "the first 55 minutes are easy" and
"the last 20 minutes are very important, you have to stay in the kitchen." This is notably
SHORTER than the Teochew base's 1½–2 hours.

> ⚠️ **His own two warnings, verbatim — don't lose these:**
> - **最後的二十分鐘非常重要。必須守在廚房。** — "The last twenty minutes are very important.
>   You must stay in the kitchen."
> - **切鴨子的時候要非常的小心。** — "Be very careful when cutting the duck."

> ⚠️ **BEFORE YOU START: have every ingredient prepared, measured and within reach** (user,
> 20 Sep). **This matters most for the dark soy sauce** — the caramelizing step gives you only
> seconds between the sugar turning golden and the sugar burning, and his instruction there is
> literally "趕快倒入醬油" ("quickly pour in the soy sauce"). If you're still measuring it out at
> that moment, the sugar burns and you have to wash the pot and start over. Measure the 100cc
> out in advance and stand it next to the stove. Same goes for the spices, the boiling water
> and the duck — all ready before the sugar goes in.

**Running adjustments, apply throughout the cook:**
- If the sauce looks like it's getting too thick at any point — check at each flip — **add half
  a cup of water, and repeat each time it happens again.** Not a one-off fix; keep doing it as
  many times as needed (user's own note).
- **If the pot starts to scorch, pour in the reserved gravy** from the 55-minute mark. Originally
  his instruction, bundled into the now-cancelled 70-minute flip step — kept here as a standing
  troubleshooting note so it isn't lost ("假如鍋燒焦了可以倒入剛才留下來的湯汁").

1. **Rub and scald:** Rub salt over the duck's body, massaging it in. **Also use salt specifically
   on the mouth and throat — scrub until the mucus/slime there is completely washed clean**, per
   his own instruction: "洗鴨子的時候要用鹽洗鴨子的嘴巴和喉嚨。那些粘液要洗乾淨。" **Near the tail
   end of the cavity, cut off and discard the small stretch of intestine still there — this is
   where the smelliest waste residue collects.** His own words: "鴨子肚子近尾巴的地方有一條小腸要
   切掉。哪是最臭的糞。" **Also cut out the preen gland (oil gland, 尾脂腺) — the pinkish nub
   just above the vent.** It's full of a musky oil that will taint the braise. **Dig out just the
   gland — do NOT cut off the whole tail** (user, 20 Sep). **Go over the skin and pull out any
   remaining feather stubs / pin feathers** — tweezers or a small pinch works; they're easy to
   miss and unpleasant to find on the plate (user, 20 Sep). Scald briefly in boiling water
   ([washing technique
   reference](https://youtu.be/wUEJKmetBck?is=OWtBqrr1eRp5N_zM)), then pour away the salted water.

2. ⭐ **COAT THE DUCK IN DARK SOY SAUCE — IMPORTANT, DON'T SKIP.** Rub dark soy sauce all over
   the cleaned duck, inside and out, to build the colour before it ever goes into the pot
   (user, 20 Sep). The braising liquid alone won't give the finished duck the depth of colour
   this dish is supposed to have — this coating is what does it. **No measured amount — just use
   enough to coat it for colour, by eye** (user, 20 Sep). Then stuff the cleaned whole piece of
   blue ginger into the duck's cavity and set the duck aside.

3. **Caramelize the sugar:** ⚠️ **Before lighting the stove, have the measured soy sauce standing
   right next to the pot** — you will not have time to fetch or measure it once the sugar starts
   colouring. Put the sugar carefully into the pot over medium heat. Wait for it to dissolve,
   stirring with a spoon, until it bubbles and turns golden yellow. Quickly pour in the soy sauce.
   Stir again, then pour in a big bowl of boiling water. **If the sugar burns at this stage, don't
   try to rescue it — wash out the pot and start the caramelizing again** (his own instruction:
   "假如開始的時候糖燒焦了，就洗掉。重新來過。").
4. **Into the pot:** Add the whole spices (cinnamon, star anise, Sichuan peppercorns) to the pot
   **raw, straight in — no toasting** — **at the same time as the duck**, per his own text, "you
   can add the spices when the duck goes into the pot." Place the
   duck in the pot, **belly facing UP** *(the opposite of the Teochew base's "breast-side down" —
   his instruction supersedes that)*. Add water to the pot, pouring
   some directly into the duck's cavity at the same time, until the water level in the pot
   reaches halfway up the duck's body.
5. **Cook:** Turn the heat to medium-high. Move the duck occasionally.
6. **First 55 minutes — "easy":**
   - After **30 min**: turn the duck over. Lower the heat a little. **Add the gizzard now** —
     confirmed by him to go in at this exact point. **Add the heart at the same time** —
     provisional, my recommendation rather than his (see Offal above).
   - After a further **25 min** (55 min total): turn again. **Take out a small cup of the gravy
     and set it aside** — this is insurance for the next phase, not a master-stock practice.
7. **Last ~20 minutes — "very important, stay in the kitchen":**
   - After a further **10 min** (65 min total): turn again. Lower the heat further.
   - **For the final ~15 minutes of the whole cook, keep the heat on LOW** (user's own note) —
     this covers the rest of this phase through to the end, refining the "lower the heat
     further" step above into a specific heat level rather than just "lower."
   - **Add the liver with about 10 minutes left** on the clock — it only needs 10 minutes to
     cook, so timing it to finish when the duck finishes avoids overcooking it. Once cooked,
     leave it sitting in the pot rather than removing it.
   - **From here to the end: keep shifting the duck constantly** so it doesn't stick to the pot
     (user, 20 Sep). This replaces the discrete timed flips that previously sat at the 70-minute
     and ~72–73-minute marks — those are cancelled.
   - Done at roughly **70–75 minutes total.**
8. **Rest & serve:** Lift the duck out. **Be very careful when cutting it** — his own emphasis,
   presumably because it's very tender by this point. *(Resting time and whether gravy is spooned
   over at the table aren't explicitly stated — presumably similar to the Teochew base.)*

### Offal cleaning, confirmed by him directly

- **Gizzard:** the yellow inner lining must be removed. Then rub with salt several times and
  rinse clean with water (same as the liver).
- **Liver:** rub with salt several times, then rinse clean with water. His 10-minute cooking
  timing already accounts for how quickly it overcooks — no need to adjust further.
- **Heart:** squeeze out the blood from inside — no need to cut it open.
