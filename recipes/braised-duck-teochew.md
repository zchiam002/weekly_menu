# Teochew Braised Duck 潮州卤鸭

> The Teochew classic — a whole duck slow-braised in dark soy with galangal, old ginger and garlic, perfumed with cinnamon, star anise and cloves until the meat pulls away from the bone. Better the next day.

**Servings:** 8

## Ingredients

### Duck

- 1 whole duck, about 2kg

### Braising liquid

- 1 cup premium dark soy sauce
- ½ cup light soy sauce
- 2 tbsp five spice powder
- 40g rock sugar

### Whole spices

- 4–5 cinnamon sticks
- 2 star anise
- 8 cloves
- 1 tsp white peppercorns
- ½ tsp Sichuan peppercorns

### Aromatics (pounded or blended)

- 100g blue ginger (galangal / lengkuas)
- 70g old ginger
- 90g garlic

## Method

1. **Prep the duck:** Clean the duck well, trim off excess fat at the cavity, and pat dry. Blanch briefly in boiling water if you want a cleaner braise, then drain.
2. **Aromatics:** Pound or blend the blue ginger, old ginger and garlic into a coarse paste.
3. **Fry:** Heat a little oil in a large deep pot and fry the aromatic paste over medium-low heat until fragrant and the raw edge is gone.
4. **Spices:** Add the cinnamon sticks, star anise, cloves, white peppercorns, Sichuan peppercorns and five spice powder. Fry briefly until aromatic.
5. **Braising liquid:** Add the dark soy sauce, light soy sauce and rock sugar, then enough water to come roughly two-thirds up the duck. Bring to a boil.
6. **Braise:** Lower the duck in breast-side down. Simmer gently, covered, turning the duck every 20–30 min and ladling the gravy over it, for about 1½–2 hours until the meat is tender and pulls easily from the bone.
7. **Rest & serve:** Lift the duck out and rest before chopping. Serve with the braising gravy spooned over. Traditionally served alongside braised tau kwa (firm beancurd) and hard-boiled eggs cooked in the same gravy.
