# Singapore Lor Mee 新加坡卤面

> Pork belly braised in dark soy and five spice, with the braising liquid built into a thick egg-ribbon gravy and ladled over noodles, bean sprouts, ngoh hiang, fish cake and shrimp dumplings.

**Servings:** Not specified in source (large pot — ~800g pork belly, 1L stock + 3L water for the gravy)

## Ingredients

### Pork & braising aromatics

- ~800g pork belly
- 4 pieces muscle meat
- 1½ cups dark soy sauce (plus 4–5 tbsp for the initial colouring)
- 1 cup rice wine (plus a little for massaging the meat)
- ½ cup light soy sauce
- 2 tsp five spice powder
- 1 tbsp white peppercorns
- 2 small pieces rock sugar (about thumb-size)
- 2 whole bulbs garlic, washed, skin left on
- 2 star anise
- 2 pieces cinnamon bark
- 10 cloves
- ~1 tbsp oil
- A little pepper
- Water, enough to cover the meat by 3–4 inches

### Gravy base & thickening

- 1L concentrated chicken stock
- 3L water
- 3 tsp salt
- 1 tbsp chicken stock bouillon / powder
- ~¼ cup tapioca flour solution (made from 1 cup tapioca flour mixed with 3 cups water)
- 3 eggs, beaten

### Noodles & condiments

- Noodles (quantity not specified)
- Bean sprouts (quantity not specified)
- Shrimp dumplings (quantity not specified)
- Crispy ngoh hiang (quantity not specified)
- Fish cake (quantity not specified)

## Method

1. **Colour and massage the meat:** Put the pork belly and muscle meat in a mixing bowl with 4–5 tbsp dark soy sauce, a little pepper and a little rice wine. Massage it in and leave skin-side down for a couple of minutes.
2. **Fry the aromatics:** Heat ~1 tbsp oil in a pan and fry the garlic, star anise, cinnamon bark, cloves and white peppercorns until they release their aroma and the garlic turns brownish. Remove the spices and garlic, leaving the oil in the pan.
3. **Brown the meat:** In the same oil, brown the meat for a couple of minutes on each side for colour.
4. **Braise the pork:** Transfer the fried spices and garlic, the browned meat, 1 cup rice wine, ½ cup light soy sauce, 1½ cups dark soy sauce, 2 tsp five spice powder, the rock sugar and enough water to cover the meat by 3–4 inches into a pot. Cover and cook for 45 minutes to 1 hour until tender, checking regularly to keep the water level up. Remove the braised pork once done.
5. **Gravy base:** To the remaining braising liquid add the concentrated chicken stock and 3L water. Cover and bring to a boil.
6. **Season and thicken:** Once boiling, add the salt and chicken stock powder. Slowly stir in about ¼ cup of the tapioca flour solution, scraping the bottom continuously to stop it burning, until thickened to your liking.
7. **Egg ribbons:** Stir the gravy and pour in the beaten eggs to form ribbons. Turn off the heat.
8. **Assemble and serve:** Blanch the noodles and bean sprouts in hot water and divide between bowls. Pour the thick gravy over, then top with sliced braised pork, shrimp dumplings, crispy ngoh hiang and fish cake.
