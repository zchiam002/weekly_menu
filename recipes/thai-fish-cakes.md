# Thai Fish Cakes (Tod Mun Pla) ทอดมันปลา

> Springy Thai fish cakes — white fish paste with red curry paste, kaffir lime leaves and long
> beans, kneaded until bouncy, then fried until puffed and golden.

**Servings:** 5.5 as a side dish (guide serves 3–4 — quantities below are pre-scaled ×1.6).
Makes about 25–30 small cakes.

## Ingredients

### Fish cakes

- 700g white fish fillets, minced — basa, tilapia or featherback. Tender fish gives a springier
  texture; avoid firm fish
- 3–5 tbsp Thai red curry paste — pick a brand that isn't overly salty; start at 3, it carries
  the salt and the heat
- 1 large egg + 1 egg yolk
- 0–1½ tbsp fish sauce — only if the test cake needs salt (see method)
- 1½ tsp sugar
- 13–16 kaffir lime leaves, spine removed, sliced paper-thin
- ¾ cup long beans, sliced into thin rounds (≈80g — estimated conversion)
- A small handful of Thai basil, about 10–15g, roughly chopped (holy basil also works)
  *(quantity estimated — the original lists it without one)*
- 1½ tbsp tapioca starch or cornflour (optional — only if the paste is wet)
- Neutral oil, for deep-frying

### To serve

- Bottled Thai sweet chilli sauce, or plum sauce — the simplest option
- *Or* the original video's homemade dipping sauce (no quantities given): spur chilli or red
  capsicum, bird's eye chillies, garlic, cane vinegar, sugar, water and salt. **Simmer it briefly**
  rather than serving it raw.
- Skip the original's raw cucumber and shallot garnish. Its roasted, ground peanuts are fine.

## Method

1. **Prep:** Stack the kaffir lime leaves, roll them tightly and slice into paper-thin strips.
   Slice the long beans into very thin rounds. Roughly chop the basil.
2. **Make the paste:** Process the fish, curry paste, egg, egg yolk and sugar in a food processor
   until completely smooth, cohesive and bouncy. (If using pre-ground fish, knead everything
   together by hand in a large bowl.) Add the starch only if the paste is wet.
3. **Knead:** Transfer to a bowl and fold in the lime leaves, long beans and basil. Knead in one
   direction for 2–3 minutes, or pick up the paste and slap it back into the bowl repeatedly —
   this is what builds the springy texture.
4. **Test cake:** Fry one small cake and taste it. Add fish sauce only if it needs more salt — the
   original video adds none, because the curry paste is already salty. *Never taste the raw paste.*
5. **Shape:** With wet or oiled hands, take 1–1½ tbsp of paste and flatten it into a patty about
   1cm thick and 5cm wide.
6. **Fry:** Heat 2.5–5cm of oil to 175°C over medium heat. Fry in batches without crowding, 2–3
   minutes, flipping halfway, until puffed and golden on both sides. *(The original video
   shallow/pan-fries instead, which uses much less oil.)*
7. **Drain** on paper towels and **serve hot.** Cut one open first: opaque and springy right through, no translucent
   paste in the centre.

> **Where the two disagree:** the guide uses a whole egg and 1 tbsp fish sauce, and deep-fries; the
> original uses only the yolk, no fish sauce, adds Thai basil, and pan-fries. This version uses

> with pan-frying as the alternative.

### Guide quantities (serves 3–4, 15–20 small cakes), for reference

450g white fish · 2–3 tbsp red curry paste · 1 large egg · 1 tbsp fish sauce · 1 tsp sugar ·
8–10 kaffir lime leaves · ½ cup long beans · 1 tbsp tapioca starch or cornstarch (optional) ·
neutral oil for deep-frying.
