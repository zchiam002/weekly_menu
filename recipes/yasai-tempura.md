# Yasai Tempura (Vegetable & Shrimp) — Chef Kunihiro

> Crispy, light homemade tempura — vegetables and shrimp in a thin, lumpy cold batter, with a tentsuyu dipping sauce. Two rules: keep everything cold to prevent gluten forming, and control the oil temperature.

> Seafood-free version: omit the shrimp and use a kombu or chicken dashi in the tentsuyu.

## Ingredients

### Tempura batter

- 100g cake flour (cold/chilled)
- 180ml cold water
- 1 large egg (cold)

> Cake flour has the lowest protein content, and cold slows gluten development — gluten makes batter heavy and soggy. Chill the flour, water and egg before starting.

### Tentsuyu (dipping sauce)

- 400ml dashi stock
- 100ml soy sauce
- 100ml mirin
- Grated daikon radish (for serving)

### Proteins & vegetables (dry veg works best)

- Shrimp (size 16–20 count/lb)
- Kabocha squash, carrots, eggplant, shiitake mushrooms, broccoli, shiso leaves
- Avoid watery veg like cucumber or tomato

## Method

### Tentsuyu dipping sauce

1. Combine the dashi, soy sauce and mirin in a small pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce and simmer 2 min to burn off the alcohol. Set aside and reheat right before serving.
2. Grate the daikon and gently squeeze out excess water in a strainer (leave it moist).

### Vegetable cuts

- **Kabocha & carrots:** slice 5 mm thick.
- **Broccoli:** small florets (so they don't burn on the pot bottom).
- **Shiitake:** remove stems; shallow decorative criss-cross cuts on top.
- **Eggplant:** split in half; make 3 partial lengthwise cuts leaving the top attached, cut through on the 4th, and fan out gently.
- **Shiso:** trim the stems.

### Shrimp prep (prevents curling & splatter)

1. Peel the shell keeping the tail intact; remove the sharp spiky shell above the tail.
2. Devein along the back. Trim the tail tips diagonally, then scrape out excess water/dirt with the knife tip.
3. Wash and pat completely dry.
4. Make 5–6 shallow (2 mm) cuts across the belly side. Place belly-down and gently press until the muscle fibres snap — this keeps the shrimp straight while frying.

### Flour coating (pre-batter)

- Dust all prepped veg and shrimp in a light layer of cake flour, tapping off excess.
- Shiso: dust the back side only.
- Shrimp: dust the meat only, not the tail.

### Make the batter

1. Sift the 100g cold cake flour.
2. Whisk the cold egg into the 180ml cold water until combined.
3. Add the sifted flour to the egg mixture. **Do not overmix** — gently push/sink the flour into the liquid with chopsticks. The batter should stay thin, watery and lumpy.

### Frying

Heat 3 cm of vegetable oil to 160–170°C (320–340°F). Test: drop in a bit of batter — if it sinks then immediately floats back up, it's ready.

| Item | Oil temp | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kabocha & carrots | 160–170°C | 2.5–3 min | Turn every minute; done when bubbles shrink and sizzling quietens |
| Shiitake | 160–170°C | 3 min | Cap-side UP first; flip once batter sets (30–40 sec) |
| Eggplant | 160–170°C | 2 min | Fan out, skin-side UP; flip every 40 sec |
| Broccoli | 160–170°C | 2.5–3 min | Smooth-side down; flip after 1 min, keep florets up most of the time |
| Shiso leaves | 160–170°C | 40 sec max | Batter back side only; front-up 30 sec, flip 10 sec, remove |
| Shrimp | 170–180°C | 1–1.5 min | Hold by tail, belly-side UP; flick extra batter over the top for crispy blooms (tenkasu) |

Serve immediately with the warm tentsuyu and grated daikon.
