## Monday (3 Aug) — Taiwanese braised pork rice 滷肉飯

## Tuesday (4 Aug) — Thai style steamed fish 泰式蒸鱼
Verdict: **CAUTION → heat the sauce; drop the raw garnish**
- **The sauce is raw** — 80g garlic, chillies, cilantro *roots*, lime, fish sauce, blended and poured over. Cilantro roots are soil-grown and hardest to clean. **Scrub hard, then boil the sauce 1–2 min**, or spoon it over for the last 2–3 min of steaming. **This is the week's one raw touchpoint that genuinely matters** (Western)
- Omit the raw lime slices and cilantro topping (pref/Western)
- **80g of raw garlic would have been killed under T1 rules. That objection is gone** — the only reason to heat it now is food safety, not smell (pref)
- **Chillies 16 → 6–8.** A heat call, not an aversion one (TCM/Western)
- Sea bass is low-mercury. Two ~550g fish steam more evenly than one large. Opaque at the spine (Western/pref)

## Tuesday (4 Aug) — Yu xiang qie zi 鱼香茄子 *(new)*
Verdict: **CAUTION → cook the mince through; do NOT deep-fry the eggplant**
- **No alcohol and no sesame oil — checked line by line.** The only savoury dish across Tue/Wed/Thu clean on both standing rules without modification (Western/pref)
- **Minced pork is the doneness item.** Mincing distributes surface bacteria through the meat, so it must cook *through*, not just sear. **Break it up completely — no clumps — and brown it before the water and sauce go in.** Grey-brown and crumbly right through (Western)
- **Do NOT deep-fry or shallow-fry the eggplant, however traditional that is.** This is load-bearing: it's the difference between Tuesday having zero fried dishes and quietly becoming a fry night again. **Steam the chunks 5–6 min, or dry-sear, then braise** (TCM/pref)
- **Keep the saltwater soak — it isn't just seasoning.** It collapses the eggplant's air pockets so it drinks far less oil. The cheapest fat lever on Tuesday (pref)
- **辣豆瓣酱 reads mildly warming 辛温, not 上火.** One tablespoon braised into a watery sauce across 5.5 adults is a long way from the deep-fried dish it replaced. **Not a heaty flag** — just don't let it climb when scaling (TCM)
- 茄子 is 凉 and 滑利 and some traditions caution it in pregnancy. **Culinary amounts of well-cooked eggplant braised with ginger, garlic and a warming chilli-bean paste are balanced by the dish itself**, and the cooling caution is T1-weight anyway. Stated plainly so it doesn't get raised later (TCM)
- Spring onion greens go into the hot sauce and are stirred through, not scattered raw (pref)
- Scale ~1.4×: ~450g eggplant, ~280g mince, 7 garlic. **Hold 辣豆瓣酱 at 1–1.5 tbsp** (pref)

## Wednesday (5 Aug) — Tofu hotplate with broccoli 鸡蛋豆腐锅 *(new)*
Verdict: **CAUTION → omit the prawns; drop the optional wine; set the egg fully**
- **OMIT the 100g prawns entirely** — standing rule, and don't substitute other seafood. Between 320g egg tofu and 200g mince the dish isn't short of protein; bump the mince if you want the volume back (pref/Western)
- **The 1 tbsp Shaoxing is marked "optional but recommended" in the recipe itself — so just leave it out.** The easiest alcohol fix on the menu: no substitution needed, the recipe already sanctions going without (Western)
- **The beaten-egg finish is fine** — egg as an ingredient *inside* a dish, not a stand-alone egg dish. The one instruction is to **set it fully**: opaque set curds and ribbons, no glossy translucent streaks (Western)
- **If it goes out on a sizzling hotplate, set the egg in the pot on the stove first, then transfer.** Pouring raw beaten egg onto the plate at the table is the one version of this dish carrying real risk — a hotplate cools faster than it looks (Western)
- **Broccoli in place of the snow peas — the recipe offers this itself. Scale right up to 250–300g:** it's Wednesday's vegetable, not a garnish (pref)
- **Wash the broccoli head-down in salted water** — florets trap grit and insects. Cook fully tender, no raw or barely-blanched florets (Western)
- **Doubanjiang at 2 tbsp plus ½ tbsp black bean paste** — still 辛温 warming rather than 上火, since this is a wet braise not a fry. But it's the **second night running for chilli-bean paste** and both pastes are very salty. **Hold at 2–2.5 tbsp even when scaling** (TCM/Western)
- Sear the egg tofu first or it falls apart in the braise. Keep chilled, within date (Western/pref)
- **Nothing is fried in this dish** — which is exactly why it works beside pre-fried noodles (TCM)
- Scale ~1.5× minus the prawns: ~480g egg tofu, ~300g mince, 250–300g broccoli, ~375ml water, 2 eggs (pref)

## Thursday (6 Aug) — Beef rendang 仁当牛肉 *(Madam Kwan's paste)*
Verdict: **CAUTION → sodium is the headline; 1.5 packets not 2**
- **3,524mg sodium per packet, 153% of a day's DV.** Two packets is ~7,000mg in the pot. **Use 1.5 packets for the full 900g–1kg of beef** — same meat, a quarter less salt, ~960mg a head. Taste at 30 min before opening the last half. **Add no salt, soy, fish sauce or stock at any stage** — the paste is a finished seasoning, not a base (Western)
- **MSG (INS 621) is not a pregnancy concern. Leave it in.** Glutamate is an ordinary amino acid; the body doesn't distinguish added from natural. Its only relevance is arithmetic — MSG is itself a sodium salt, already inside the count above (Western)
- **INS 211 and INS 202 don't matter either.** Both cleared at food levels by every major regulator, neither on any obstetric avoid-list. **No action** (Western)
- **The crustacean allergen is an approved cooked background flavouring — keep it.** Doesn't breach the no-whole-prawns rule, doesn't make this a fish night (pref/Western)
- **Candlenut is a tree nut and a non-issue** — avoiding nuts in pregnancy doesn't prevent allergy in the child, and it's cooked twice over. An allergen declaration only (Western)
- **Beef doneness: 50 min is enough — but only if it's actually sliced. Buy chuck, not shin**, sliced ~5mm across the grain. **Chunks means 1.5–2 hrs.** Grey-brown right through, pulling apart under chopstick pressure (Western)
- **The week's only red meat and its best iron.** Heme iron absorbs several times better than plant iron, and T2 is when blood volume expands. **Eat it alongside the green, and no strong tea with this meal** — tannins cut absorption (Western/TCM)
- **UHT packet coconut milk**, or fresh-pressed the same day. Refrigerate leftovers promptly (Western)
- **The paste is far milder than the scratch rendang it replaced** — chilli sits behind shallots (26%), palm oil, garlic (13%) and curry powder, and there's no kerisik or warming spice cluster. **The "pull chillies back to 8–10" instruction is retired — there's nothing left to pull back** (TCM)
- Rich rather than heaty: palm oil second ingredient plus 500ml coconut milk. Serve earlier — a common T2 reflux trigger (TCM/Western)

## Friday (7 Aug) — Hakka mei cai kou rou 梅菜扣肉
Verdict: **CAUTION → soak the mei cai properly; steam it long**
- **The 2 tbsp rice wine in the poaching liquid needs NO action** — open poach, and the liquid is **discarded entirely** (Western)
- **The soak-and-rinse does three jobs:** pulls out curing salt, removes sand (mei cai is sun-dried and famously gritty — unwashed soil is the actual pathogen vector), and carries away water-soluble nitrite. **Soak 20–30 min in 3+ changes of water, rinse until clear.** One quick rinse is not enough (Western)
- **On nitrites, concretely:** mustard greens are naturally high in nitrate, which converts to nitrite during pickling — but that **peaks early and falls away** once fully cured and dried. A commercial dried packet is long past it, and split across 5.5 adults the quantity is small. **Not a reason to drop the dish.** Buy a fully cured commercial packet, not a young or home-pickled batch. The gastric-cancer epidemiology concerns habitual heavy intake, not one meal (Western)
- **Buy 甜梅菜 sweet, not 咸梅菜 salty** — meaningfully lower salt (pref)
- **Steam a full 60–90 min.** The poach cooks it, the fry only blisters the skin — the steam makes it tender (Western)
- **Pat the skin bone-dry before it goes in the oil and use a splatter lid.** Poking holes then lowering into hot oil spatters violently — the likeliest injury of the week (pref)
- **The week's one genuinely 湿热 dish.** Hold at 600g (700g ceiling if the table looks thin) (TCM/pref)

## What changed vs the earlier version of this menu

**Substantially better, in the ways that were actually being flagged.** The previous version's two headline findings were a four-night heaty run and a two-night sodium pair. **This revision retires the first outright and halves the second.**

Removing the twice-deep-fried sticky honey pork and the shallow-fried tod mun pla took both in-kitchen frying nights off the board, leaving one — Friday's kou rou skin. Tuesday went from the heaviest night of the week to one of the lightest: a steamed fish, a braise, and a four-hour bone soup. Dropping the tonjiru removed ~6,000–7,000mg of sodium from Friday in a single move — a larger cut than every prep adjustment in the last vet combined.

**Vegetable coverage is the quietest and possibly most valuable improvement.** The last vet had to write that Thursday's green was "still the only dedicated vegetable dish on the menu." That sentence is retired: four of five nights now carry a real vegetable.

**Two honest debits, both small.** Friday lost its counterweight — the tonjiru's daikon was doing 消食下气 work aimed at fried-then-steamed pork belly, and nothing replaced it; the fix is to make Friday's vegetable the biggest of the week. And Wednesday is now the wine night, with four of six actionable alcohol instances on one evening.

**Net: the previous version was a heavy menu being managed carefully. This is a moderate menu with one salty night, one rich night, one fry, and better vegetables — and it needs less managing to be safe.**
