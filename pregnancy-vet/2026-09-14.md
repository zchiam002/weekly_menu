## Monday (14 Sep) — Buta don 豚丼
Verdict: **AVOID as written — the optional raw egg yolks must come off**
- **⚠️ THE ONE REAL AVOID OF THE WEEK. The recipe's optional topping is "6–7 RAW EGG YOLKS,
  one per bowl."** Raw egg is on the high-risk avoid list at every trimester — salmonella.
  **OMIT them.** If the yolk matters to the dish, **the inventory carries a separate
  `Pasteurised eggs` line** — that is exactly what it is for, and it is the only safe way to
  serve a raw yolk (Western)
- **Japanese mayonnaise is fine** — commercial Kewpie is made with pasteurised egg. It is
  home-made mayo that is the problem, and this is not that (Western)
- **390ml of alcohol — 195ml sake plus 195ml mirin — the largest in the collection.** Permitted
  by your override. What still matters is the method: step 6 reduces the marinade "over low heat
  until glossy". **Take it further than glossy — a genuine hard reduction, not a gentle thickening.**
  A low simmer to syrup drives off far less than a rolling one (Western)
- **Sear in a single layer without overlapping, in batches — the recipe says so and it is also
  the doneness instruction.** 1.3kg of pork belly crowded into one skillet steams, and grey
  steamed pork belly reads as cooked while thicker slices are not (Western)
- **30g of shredded nori — a note, not an action.** Seaweed is iodine-dense and excess iodine in
  pregnancy is a real thyroid concern. 30g of nori spread across seven bowls is a fraction of the
  simmered kombu this vet has twice removed from soups, so it stays. Do not add more (Western)
- **Quantities are already scaled to 6.5 in the file** — do not scale again (pref)
- Child: buta don is Monday's dish, entirely non-spicy

## Summary

**Dishes to swap:** None.

**Dishes to adjust preparation:** Monday buta don (**omit the raw yolks**, reduce the glaze hard,
sear in batches) · Monday watercress soup (blanch and discard, watercress fully cooked) ·
Tuesday beef noodle (**ladle the child's bowl before the chilli**) · Wednesday curry (cut the
thickest piece) · Thursday meatballs (cut one open, brown in batches) · Thursday tonjiru (wilt the
onion in, miso off the boil) · Friday threadfin (**freeze Sunday, thaw Thursday**) · Friday sweet
and sour pork (**delete the sesame oil, raise the pork to 500–600g**).

**Ingredients Mia should flag:**
- **RAW EGG — one instance and it is the week's only AVOID:** buta don's optional yolks. Omit, or
  use the pasteurised eggs.
- **SESAME OIL — one instance:** Friday's sweet and sour pork marinade. Delete. **The rule was not
  lifted**, and sesame oil sits in the cupboard within reach of Thursday's steamed pomfret, which
  is the classic dish for it.
- **ALCOHOL — three instances, all permitted this week:** buta don 390ml (reduce hard), beef
  noodle ~160ml (long braise, genuinely fine), sweet and sour pork 1 tbsp (fried off). Plus one
  unwritten: **ask whether Mia's threadfin uses wine.**
- **TOMATO — two instances, both permitted:** the beef noodle's paste and fruit, the sweet and
  sour gravy.
- **MUSHROOM — zero instances, confirmed.** Do not let shiitake into the watercress soup or the
  tonjiru out of habit.
- **RAW-GARNISH TRAPS, three:** tonjiru's spring onion "to serve" · the pomfret's shreds (pass only
  under genuinely smoking oil) · the sweet and sour capsicum if added late.
- **DONENESS:** Thursday's meatballs cut open, no pink · both fish at the SPINE · Friday's battered
  pork cut open · the curry's thickest chicken piece · buta don seared in a single layer.
- **NO STRONG TEA** on Monday and Tuesday — the week's two iron nights.

**Net assessment:**

**One genuine AVOID and it is easily removed.** Buta don's raw egg yolks are optional in the
recipe, and the household already stocks pasteurised eggs, so this costs nothing. Everything else
this week is preparation-level.

**The child rule fails on Tuesday**, and that is the finding worth acting on. Taiwanese beef noodle
soup is spicy and it is the only thing on the table. Every other night has a non-spicy protein —
buta don, the fishball soup, the pomfret, the threadfin — but Tuesday has nothing. The broth is
strained, so ladling the child's bowl before the chilli goes in is a real fix rather than a fudge.

**The week is iron-rich in a way the last one was not.** Braised beef shank on Tuesday is haem iron
and the single best source in the rotation; Monday's rib soup at a proper 1.2kg does what the
reduced 250g portions could not do last week. Nothing here needs the caveat the 7 Sep vet had to
give.

**Pork appears on four of five nights** — buta don, the rib soup, the tonjiru, the sweet and sour —
though only two are mains. Monday carries it twice in one sitting, which the user had explicitly
objected to a day earlier and then reversed. Worth one more look, since it is a preference the user
stated rather than anything this vet would raise on safety grounds.

**Five of the thirteen dishes have no recipe** — the pomfret, the fishball soup, the threadfin with
leek and both greens. That is the highest proportion of any week vetted so far, and it means Mia is
improvising nearly half the table. The two worth writing down while the week is running are the
pomfret and the threadfin, both of which have now appeared repeatedly with nothing on file.

**On the lifted rules:** tomato carries no Western food-safety or TCM-stage concern in pregnancy at
all — that aversion was always a preference. Alcohol is different: it is a genuine clinical caution,
and lifting it puts the responsibility on the cooking. Of the three instances this week, the beef
noodle's is fine on its own merits, the sweet and sour pork's is trivial, and **only the buta don's
390ml depends on the reduction actually being taken far enough.**
