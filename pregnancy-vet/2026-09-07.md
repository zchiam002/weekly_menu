## Monday (7 Sep) — Stir fried bee hoon
Verdict: **CAUTION → the char siew is the whole finding**
- **CHAR SIEW IS READY-TO-EAT CHILLED PORK — the week's clearest listeria item.** It is bought
  cooked from a stall, kept cold, and normally scattered over the noodles at the very end, which
  gives it almost no heat. **Put it in WITH the cabbage so it gets real wok time and reaches
  steaming right through** (Western)
- **⚠️ THE RECIPE FILE HAS NO METHOD AND LISTS NO OIL AT ALL** — an ingredient list and a link.
  Every timing, the noodle soak and all 3–4 tbsp of oil are this vet's construction (pref)
- **RAW-GARNISH TRAP, and it is not a recipe step:** fried bee hoon reaches the table with cut raw
  chilli in soy and often raw spring onion. Neither appears in the file, both are habit. Keep the
  raw chilli saucer off the table entirely (Western)
- **Two batches, not one.** ~2.2kg of wet weight against a domestic wok's 1–1.2kg. A crowded wok
  steams rather than fries and hides under-heated char siew — the safety point and the texture
  point are the same point here (Western)
- **4 eggs beaten through the noodles is PERMITTED** — the rule bars egg as a dish, not egg cooked
  into one. Flagged so nobody removes them by mistake (pref)
- **Habit risks, all within arm's reach:** sesame oil, dried shiitake, curry powder. Despite the
  "Sin Chew" name this version has no curry powder, and it must stay that way for the child (pref)
- Child: this is Monday's dish. Lift the portion out before the last soy, chop the prawns small

- **MUSHROOM — Friday's ee-fu mee ONLY, by your override.** Tuesday's yam soup is the pot where

- **TOMATO — zero instances, confirmed dish by dish.**

- **TWO READY-TO-EAT CURED MEATS, and they are this week's listeria pair:** Monday's **char siew**
  and Wednesday's **jin hua ham**. Both normally go in at the end. Both must get real heat.
- **RAW-GARNISH TRAPS, four, none of them a recipe step:** the bee hoon's raw chilli saucer ·

- **DONENESS:** minced pork cut open, no pink · threadfin at the SPINE · sliced pork no pink in
  two passes · char siew and jin hua ham steaming through · fried-rice eggs in firm curds.
- **NO STRONG TEA on the four soup nights.**

**Net assessment:**

**This is the gentlest week on record and by some distance.** There is **no deep-frying and no
grilling anywhere in it** — the first such week in the log. Against the 17 Aug vet's sharpest
criticism, that two dry nights fell back-to-back with no soup and no green, this week runs four
long-simmered soups and greens on four of five nights. On the TCM side there is essentially
nothing to say about 燥热 or damp: it is steaming, simmering and wok work throughout.

**The one thing that genuinely concerns me is the rib reduction.** Four rib soups reads as an
exceptionally strong iron week, and that was true at 3.3kg. At **1kg across four pots** — roughly
250g of bone-in rib each, mostly bone — the soups become flavour broths rather than sources of
haem iron. Late second trimester is precisely when maternal iron demand climbs steepest. The red
dates and goji still do their 补血 work, but they are not a substitute for the meat. Nothing here
is unsafe; the week simply delivers considerably less iron than its shape suggests. **The salmon
and the 400g of minced pork are now carrying more of that load than the four soups are** — worth
knowing, and worth a word with the GP if iron levels have been borderline at any point.

**Three quantities do not exist anywhere.** The steamed threadfin has no recipe file at all; the
fishball minced pork soup has a full method and not one number; both Thursday and Friday's
vegetables are unwritten. Every figure for those came from this vet or the ingredient pass. That
is four of the week's fourteen dishes running on invented amounts — the same pattern the 17 Aug
vet hit with the krapow, and the reason it is worth asking Mia for her versions and writing them
into `recipes/` while the week is fresh.

**On the mushroom override:** it costs nothing clinically. Cooked mushrooms carry no Western
food-safety flag in pregnancy and no TCM caution at this stage. The rule has been an aversion
rather than a restriction, and suspending it for one dish is a preference decision, not a safety
one.
