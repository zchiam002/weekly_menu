## Tuesday (11 Aug) — Braised mushrooms with spinach 北菇扒菠菜

## Wednesday (12 Aug) — Yang Zhou fried rice 扬州炒饭
Verdict: **CAUTION → the day-old rice is the week's most important instruction**
- **Sesame oil, 2.5 tbsp across two fries — the largest sesame quantity on the menu, and it's used as the actual frying medium. Neutral oil throughout.** The easiest to reach for out of habit (pref)
- **THE DAY-OLD RICE. Spread it thin, fridge it within an hour of cooking, use within 24 hours. Do not leave the pot out overnight.** The *Bacillus cereus* toxin is **heat-stable** — frying it hot the next day does not undo a bad cool-down. The control is entirely in the cooling, not the reheat (Western)
- **Jin hua ham is uncooked cured ham — the Chinese prosciutto. Dice it small and cook it through in the hot wok, never stir it in off the heat.** The recipe gives no quantity: use ~60–80g. **There is no char siew in this recipe — don't buy any** (Western)
- **Prawns fully opaque — loose C, not tight O.** ~150g of the 0.5kg bought; **freeze the surplus the day it comes home** (Western)
- **7 eggs fully set into firm curds** before the rice goes in — no glossy streaks (Western)
- **Fry in two batches.** 1.2× of an already-large fried rice overloads a domestic wok and steams instead of frying (pref)
- **No alcohol in this recipe** (Western)
- Rotation note, made once: 12 days after its last cook against a 28-day cool-down. Approved (pref)

## Wednesday (12 Aug) — Cereal pork chop 麦片排骨 *(first vet)*
Verdict: **CAUTION → cook the egg yolk to dry strands; fry in small batches**
- **The raw egg yolk into hot butter, answered plainly: yes, it gets cooked, and this is one of the safer versions of that step.** The yolk hits foaming butter and is broken into fine strands — an enormous surface-area ratio, setting almost on contact. **But 20g of butter is a shallow film and the wok drops to low for the cereal, so the real risk isn't a raw yolk — it's undercooked strands hiding inside the coating** (Western)
- **The instruction: butter properly hot and foaming BEFORE the yolk goes in; stir continuously until the strands are opaque, golden and dry-looking with no glossy streaks; only THEN add the cereal.** If the yolk coats the pan instead of stranding, the wok is too cool. **Pasteurised eggs make the question disappear entirely** (Western)
- **The egg white in the marinade is a non-issue** — deep fryer at ~175°C, sealed in a cornflour crust (Western)
- **1 tbsp rice wine in the marinade → deep fryer: LEAVE AS WRITTEN, no substitution.** Ethanol boils at 78°C, the fryer runs at ~175°C, the wine is diluted into 4 tbsp of water, and the pork is thin. **Effectively nothing reaches the plate — a "do nothing" instance, stated as confidently as the corrections** (Western)
- **Butter needs no flag** — the dairy rule is about unpasteurised milk and soft cheeses. Block butter is pasteurised and it's being melted (Western)
- **Fry in small batches, oil back to temperature between.** Crowding collapses the oil temperature and gives pale, greasy, undercooked pork (Western)
- **Drain on a rack, not paper on a plate** — the cheapest 湿 reduction available (TCM/pref)
- **Instant cereal is already sweetened. Don't scale sugar and salt linearly** — hold at 3 tsp sugar and 1.5 tsp salt (Western/pref)

## Thursday (13 Aug) — Braised pork ribs *(first vet)*
Verdict: **CAUTION → half the wine, then hard-boil it uncovered before the lid**
- **ONE CUP of Chinese rice wine — by far the largest single alcohol quantity in the whole recipe collection.** The previous record was the ½ cup in the Taiwanese braise. This deserves a real instruction, not a shrug (Western)
- **Being fair to the recipe: this is the best case for burn-off** — a long covered braise then an uncovered reduction (Western)
- **But the lid is the problem. Covered simmering traps ethanol vapour and condenses it back into the pot.** You only reach trace levels after a couple of hours of largely *uncovered* simmering, and from a full cup even a small retained fraction is a bigger absolute amount than anything else this week (Western)
- **THE INSTRUCTION: ½ cup rice wine + ½ cup stock. At the deglaze, pour it in on high heat with the pot UNCOVERED and hard-boil a full 5+ minutes** — not "let it bubble" — until the sharp alcohol smell is gone and it's reduced by half. **Only then** add the seasonings, water and lid (Western)
- **Do not replace the whole cup with water** — the wine does genuine deglazing and tenderising work; halving is enough (pref)
- **Hoisin, star anise and cinnamon need NO action.** The TCM caution on 肉桂 concerns 大热 at herbal-medicine dosage — grams of powdered bark taken as medicine — not one stick in a braise (Western/TCM)
- **Sodium is the real headline, not the wine.** 2 tbsp light + 2 tbsp dark soy + hoisin + oyster for 500g of ribs, then **reduced to a glaze, which concentrates all of it.** Scaling to 800g–1kg: **go 1.5× on the soy, not 2×, and taste BEFORE the final reduction — you can add salt, you can't un-reduce it** (Western)
- **Braise a genuine 1.5–2 hours.** "Until tender" is unspecified; don't cut it to 45 min (Western)
- **Genuinely good for T2** — heme iron plus marrow and collagen from a slow extraction (TCM/Western)

## Friday (14 Aug) — Sticky honey pork *(first vet)*
Verdict: **CAUTION → do not shortcut the first fry; check at the bone**
- **1 tbsp rice wine in the marinade, sealed under cornflour → deep fryer, twice: LEAVE AS WRITTEN.** Addressing the cornflour point directly, since that's the reason to hesitate: the crust does trap moisture, but ethanol boils at 78°C and the pork goes into oil at 170–190°C twice. **Anything volatile is driven out as steam — the bubbling in the oil literally is that happening.** Deep-frying is the most aggressive alcohol-removal method in the kitchen (Western)
- **Bone-in and twice-fried is the real doneness point.** The method is designed so the **first** fry cooks it through and the second only crisps. **Do not shortcut the first fry — the second will not rescue it** (Western)
- **Meat next to the bone is last to cook and exactly where pink hides. Cut a thick piece open at the bone before the second fry** (Western)
- **Honey needs NO action, and this vet retires the misconception for good.** The warning is infant botulism in babies under 12 months. The mother's gut handles the spores and they don't cross the placenta — and it's simmered into a sauce here anyway (Western)
- **The week's most 燥热 dish.** Drain on a rack, serve immediately, serve early (TCM/Western)
- **Do NOT scale up — hold at 600g.** Friday already carries pork belly in the tonjiru. **The dish to scale on Friday is the chap chye** (pref/TCM)

## Summary

**Dishes to swap:** None. Every finding is preparation-level.

**Ingredients Mia should flag:**
- **COOKING WINE — five instances, three need action, two do not.** (1) Tue braised mushrooms 1 tbsp — **in early, bubble off uncovered, or use mushroom water.** (2) Wed cereal pork chop marinade → fryer — **NO ACTION.** (3) Thu steamed fish sauce 0.5 tbsp — **boil the sauce 30–60 sec.** (4) **Thu braised ribs, ONE CUP — half wine half stock, then 5+ min hard uncovered boil before the lid.** (5) Fri sticky honey pork marinade → twice-fried — **NO ACTION.** *Clean: steamed chicken, radish soup, fried rice, chap chye, tonjiru.*
- **Thursday is the wine night** — two of five instances, including the largest in the collection.
- **SESAME OIL — exactly three places, all out, all Tue and Wed:** steamed chicken 1 tbsp (replace with neutral, it's the only fat), braised mushrooms ½ tbsp, **Yang Zhou 2.5 tbsp used as the frying medium.** Thursday and Friday are completely sesame-free — **and the tonjiru must not acquire any.**
- **Wednesday's day-old rice is the week's single most important instruction.** Fridge within an hour, use within 24 hours. The toxin is heat-stable.
- **Jin hua ham is uncooked cured ham** — dice small, cook through. ~60–80g. **No char siew.**
- **Keep:** the dried scallops (Tue) and dried shrimp (Fri). Neither counts as a fish night — **Thursday's threadfin is the week's only one.**
- **Doneness:** chicken in a single layer; **the cereal pork chop's yolk — dry opaque strands before the cereal**; fried-rice eggs fully set; prawns opaque through; **threadfin at the SPINE**; **sticky honey pork cut open AT THE BONE before the second fry**; tonjiru's belly simmered through.
- **Sodium — Thursday and Friday are the salty nights, both adjustable.** Thu: 1.5× the soy not 2×, taste before the reduction. Fri: miso 60–70g, cubes *or* dashi, no soy in the chap chye.
- **Needs NO action, stated so nobody raises them:** butter · **honey** · curry leaves · hoisin · star anise and cinnamon at culinary quantity · **gobo** · miso · MSG · one deseeded chilli.
- **Drain fried food on a rack, not paper.** Both fry nights.
- **Serve Wednesday and Friday earlier** — fried and glazed dishes are a common T2 reflux trigger.
- **No strong tea with Tuesday's or Thursday's meals** — the week's two best iron nights.

**Net assessment:**
Nothing comes off this menu; three of the four cooking days need only small mechanical corrections. Two of four nights are steamed, there's a vegetable every night, both soups are nourishing rather than merely present, and there's no raw component beyond spring onion that a hot pot or hot oil handles by itself.

**Three things carry real weight.** Thursday's **cup of rice wine** — a lid traps ethanol vapour and returns it to the pot, so a full cup deserves the concrete fix rather than a shrug. Wednesday's **day-old rice**, where the control sits entirely in the cool-down, not the reheat. And Wednesday's **egg yolk**, which does get cooked, but only if the butter is properly hot and the strands are dry before the cereal buries them.

Two fresh finds the brief didn't anticipate: **Thursday's fish sauce carries an extra ½ tbsp of Shaoxing**, making Thursday a two-wine night; and **the Yang Zhou uses 2.5 tbsp of sesame oil as its frying medium** — so that substitution improves the dish as well as honouring the aversion.

**On heat and damp, honestly: two deep-fry nights out of four is a higher proportion than last week, and that shouldn't be softened.** But the counterweights are already built in and need nothing added — the fries are separated by Thursday's steamed fish rather than falling back-to-back, and every fry night has a wet dish on the same table. The debit is that a four-day week has no spare night, so **the sizing instructions matter more than usual: make Wednesday's vegetable and Friday's chap chye the biggest things on their tables.**

**Pork lands on three of four cooking days**, plus Tuesday's soup. No safety issue — every pork dish here is deep-fried, long-braised or simmered. **The real consequence is nutritional: there's no red meat this week, and T2 is when iron demand peaks.** So Tuesday's rib-and-red-date soup and Thursday's braised ribs carry it — keep the dates and goji generous, make Thursday's green dark, and keep strong tea away from both meals.
